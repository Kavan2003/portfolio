---
title: "Matter out of place"
description: "Products have dirt too."
date: 2026-11-06
---

Dirt is matter out of place. Mary Douglas, an anthropologist, wrote that in 1966. Soil in the garden is soil. Soil on the kitchen floor is dirt. Same thing, wrong category.

Products have dirt too.

Some apps ask for the location before the user has anything in it. Creepy. Same goes with linkedIn Stories; if you remember disappearing posts copied from Instagram dropped to a professional network, it looks weird.

Creepy, weird, spammy. It’s in the wrong category. It asks for permission before trust. The good products gain user trust as a first step. But Duolingo is a game inside a language course, and it works. So who decides the place? The core need does. People quit languages because it’s boring. The game serves the need, so it’s in place.

The opportunity: people can’t see their own core need.

The thing you like is solving a need you don’t realise.

At work, a product with limited supply. Preorder customers asked for one thing: transparency on when they’d get it. The honest answer was a year or more. Transparency was never the real need. Hope was. We built a progress bar that moves towards completion at a steady rate. No date. Hope.

Reels are the same. People watch a reel about how bad reels are, and keep scrolling. It keeps the brain busy, engaged, stimulated. That’s a need. Better ways exist; none this cheap.

The progress bar and the reels are fundamentally the same.

Here’s my core take: A builder’s job is to find the core need, solve at the cheapest rate of what the user values. If they value time: least time. That’s the only question.

→ New products that start with “how to solve this problem” often find it hard to survive later competition.

The real question: how do I make something that solves the core need at the cheapest currency user values time; convenience and money is a category thing that world optimises for?

Market research is finding what dirt is and what is food on the dining table.
